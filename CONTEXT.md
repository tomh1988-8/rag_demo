# Walford Graph

EastEnders characters, family relationships, and story events, including marriages, divorces, deaths, crimes, imprisonment, affairs, and scandals.

## Language

**Character**:
A fictional person in EastEnders, such as Phil Mitchell. A character is distinct from the actor portraying them.
_Avoid_: Actor, performer

**Core character**:
A character selected for broad coverage of their relationships and story history.

**Supporting character**:
A character included to explain evidenced relationships or events involving core characters, whose own history may have only partial coverage.
_Avoid_: Minor character, guest character

**Family tree**:
A representation of biological parentage and ancestry between characters. Adoptive and step-parent ties are retained as character relationships outside the family tree.
_Avoid_: Household, all family relationships

**Character relationship**:
A supported family or personal tie between characters, including biological, adoptive, and step-parent ties, as well as current or former marriages, partnerships, and affairs.

**Character connection**:
An evidenced link or chain of links between characters through relationships or shared story events. Sharing a theme alone does not establish a connection.

**Story event**:
An occurrence in the EastEnders narrative, such as a marriage, divorce, affair, pregnancy, death, crime, or court proceeding. Event types describe what happened; themes describe the subjects explored.

**Theme**:
A subject explored by a story event or storyline, such as domestic abuse or the manosphere. An event may concern multiple themes.
_Avoid_: Event type, character connection

**Crime**:
A criminal act established as having been committed within the story, regardless of whether the perpetrator is caught, accused, or convicted.
_Avoid_: Accusation, conviction, imprisonment

**Criminal justice involvement**:
A character's role in an accusation, arrest, trial, conviction, acquittal, imprisonment, release, or related criminal justice process. Involvement does not by itself establish that the character committed a crime.
_Avoid_: Imprisonment (as an umbrella term), guilt

**Unclassified fact**:
An evidenced story fact that fits none of the current event or relationship categories. It remains part of the supported lore even though no agreed category describes it.

**Claim**:
An assertion about a character, relationship, or story event tied to the source evidence from which it was obtained. A claim may be checked, disputed, or corrected rather than treated as an established fact.
_Avoid_: Established fact (before checking)

**Source-supported fact**:
A statement about EastEnders supported by an identifiable passage in a source. This status alone does not establish independent corroboration.

**Cross-checked fact**:
A source-supported story fact checked against additional independent evidence and found consistent. Matching identifiers or repeated copies of an account do not by themselves constitute that check.
_Avoid_: Verified (without an actual evidence check)

**Story event date**:
The date or period when an event occurs within the fictional world of EastEnders. Its timing may be exact, approximate, known only relative to another event, or unknown.
_Avoid_: Broadcast date, source publication date
