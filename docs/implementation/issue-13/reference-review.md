# Crime and criminal justice source review, before extraction

The six excerpts in `data/seed/crime-pilot-v1.json` were read against the
unchanged captured `core-profiles-v2` paragraphs before any model call. The
reference records exact original paragraph/document offsets, source revisions,
capture hashes, attribution and licences. All seven identities reuse the reviewed
captured catalogue; no performer is a character. The selected snapshot is a
traceable excerpt collection, not a new source capture or independent canon check.

Seventeen source assertions describe three incidents. Lucy's case separates
Ben's arrest, Max's arrest/charge, conviction and later imprisonment, the narrator's
revelation naming Bobby as killer, and Max's separate accusation of Bobby. Only
the narrator's revelation supports commission; Max's conviction supplies no such
support. The earlier legal account is retained alongside the later revelation.

Stella's death excerpt explicitly describes her jumping, followed by Phil's claim
to have killed her and release without charge. That independent narrative supports
the false-confession label; release alone would not. No suicide is classified as a
crime. No formal arrest is inferred merely from the wording 'taken into custody'.

Carl's crash excerpt explicitly describes framing, Max's arrest, release on bail,
coerced admission to his family, trial and release without charge. Another captured
paragraph describes acquittal. Those source accounts are retained as distinct legal
assertions; acquittal does not establish innocence. The false allegation/confession
labels rely on the independent framing narrative. Remand following Max's separate
alleged assault on Carl is deliberately outside this incident. Ian's absence from
trial does not establish that he actually testified.

Dates remain unknown except the explicitly relative 'Later on' and 'On the day of
Max's trial'. Storyline headings do not establish calendar dates. Tests additionally
exercise synthetic exact, approximate and relative timing, role/direction errors,
conflicts, negation and unresolved identities. Synthetic tests are not canon labels.

The annotations cover in-catalogue assertions about only these named incidents.
Other nearby acts (jury bribery, kidnapping, abuse and the separate assault) are
outside scope. The extractor receives that incident scope and the public excerpts,
with names/aliases/performers and its schema; expected roles, claim labels and
review notes are withheld. Extraction results will be scored against this frozen
reference without repairing raw values or labels to improve a score.

This is exposed development material reviewed by Codex independently of the
extraction model's future output, without human adjudication or independent canon
corroboration. Independent completion review will also inspect the labels and final
answers. Wider corrections, disputes, held-out quality and release remain later
obligations.
